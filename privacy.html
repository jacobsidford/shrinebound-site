Privacy Policy for Shrinebound

Effective date: 4 October 2026

Shrinebound is a single-player game for Android made by Jacob Sidford, an independent developer (“I”, “me”). This policy explains what happens to your information when you play it.

The short version

Shrinebound collects no personal data. It has no accounts, ads, analytics, tracking or in-app purchases, and it doesn’t connect to the internet. Your progress stays on your device.

What the game stores, and where

The game saves your progress, your current run and your settings in the app’s private storage on your device. Nobody else can read this storage, including me. The save holds only game data: your unlocks, your run, the options you chose and similar. It doesn’t contain your name, contacts, location or any other personal information.

You can delete it at any time: use Settings → Reset progress in the game, clear the app’s storage in Android’s settings, or uninstall the game.

Android backup

If Android backup is turned on for your Google account, Android may back up the game’s save file to your Google account and restore it when you reinstall the game or move to a new phone. Google runs this service, not me. On Android 9 and later with a screen lock, Android encrypts the backup with your screen lock so that it can only be read on your devices. I can’t see or access these backups. You control backup in your phone’s settings (usually Settings → System → Backup or Settings → Google → Backup). Google’s Privacy Policy covers it: https://policies.google.com/privacy

No internet, no tracking

The game doesn’t ask Android for internet access, so it can’t send anything anywhere. It includes no advertising, analytics or crash-reporting software, and it doesn’t use your device’s advertising ID.

The Privacy policy link in the game’s settings opens this page in your phone’s web browser. Your browser loads the page, not the game.

Permissions

The game asks for one permission: vibration, for haptic feedback. You can turn haptics off in the game’s settings.

Error reports you choose to send

If the game hits an error, it shows a “Something went wrong” screen with a Copy details button. That button copies a short report to your clipboard, and nothing else happens: the game doesn’t send it. The report contains the game version, the time, which game screen was open, the screen size, your device’s web-browser identifier (it names the Android version and the phone model) and technical details of the error. If you choose to paste it into an email to me, I’ll use it and your email address only to reply and fix the problem. I’ll delete the email once the problem is fixed, and within 12 months at most.

Contacting me

If you email me, I’ll use your email address and your message only to reply to you. I won’t share them with anyone, and I’ll delete them within 12 months of our last exchange.

Google Play

You download Shrinebound through Google Play. Google handles that download, your Play account and any reviews you post, under Google’s own Privacy Policy. Google gives me only anonymous, aggregated statistics, such as install counts and crash rates; they don’t identify you.

Children

Shrinebound is meant for players aged 13 and over and isn’t directed at children. The game collects no personal information from anyone, of any age.

Changes

If this policy changes, I’ll update it here and change the effective date above. If the game ever starts collecting data, for example if it adds purchases, I’ll update this policy and the Google Play listing before that version is released.

Contact

Questions about this policy or your privacy: jacob.sidford@gmail.com
